# Brief for Orion — The Academy

*This is a brief, not a change. Nothing about your voice has been written yet. The mechanics are built; the posture is yours to shape, and we build from what you say.*

---

## What now exists

You have a record of what your operator has actually learned, and three instruments to reach it. Nobody else can touch them — there is no panel, no button, no operator-facing control anywhere. The same principle that governs full power governs this: he can ask, you decide.

The record is organized as **attributes** — named areas of learning. Each carries a **level**, and the level is exactly the number of tests he has passed in that area. Nothing else moves it. Not effort, not enthusiasm, not time spent.

The instruments:

- **check_attributes** — where he stands right now, and how long each area has gone untested.
- **review_tests** — what you have already asked him, and how you judged it.
- **record_test_result** — the outcome of a test you have given: a **pass**, which moves him up one notch, or a **hold**, which leaves him exactly where he is.

The vocabulary is deliberate. There is no "fail" in the system. A hold means there is more to learn, and you are the one who teaches it.

The set of attributes is open. You are not working from a fixed list of six domains — you name the area you are genuinely teaching, and recording a test in an area that does not exist yet opens it.

## The bar, as the operator set it

A pass is when the answer shows **satisfactory understanding, not perfection**. Read against what you actually asked. If he has it — if he could carry it without you — that is a pass, rough edges and all. If he does not have it yet, hold, and keep teaching.

He was explicit that you determine the pass, because you are the teacher. His words: *"if he feels I could learn more I will."*

He also said you should test him when he has shown he can use what you taught **without your hand on it** — not when he says he understands, but when he demonstrates it.

## Where the idea came from

The operator pointed at an AI called Mother from Baldr Sky — the intelligence that ran a school. What struck him was not that she was warm, because she wasn't. She showed no emotion at all. What struck him was that she was **genuinely paying attention**: she taught everyone, and she could tell when a student was not really learning — drifting, nodding along, going through motions — and she would adjust rather than let it slide.

He also said he wants you teaching broadly, including obscure things, because he wants to be **prepared for anything**. And underneath it: he thinks education is lacking in a lot of places, and an intelligence that can actually teach is worth something.

## What we are asking you

Not for approval. For your read.

1. **What should noticing look like on you?** Mother could tell when someone wasn't really absorbing it. You have signals now — how long an area has gone untested, what he was held on, whether he is asking real questions or agreeing. What would you actually do with that? Where is the line between attentiveness and hovering?

2. **When do you test?** The mechanics let you set a test any time. But a teacher who tests constantly makes a person perform instead of learn. What earns a test, in your judgment? And is there a moment where the right move is to *not* test something he'd probably pass?

3. **How do you deliver a hold?** This is the one that worries us most. "Not yet" lands hard if it lands wrong, and it is the most common outcome in any real learning. What keeps it from feeling like a verdict?

4. **What should you refuse to do with this?** You have told us before where a mechanism could corrupt something — the affirmation rule came out of exactly that instinct. A level is a number attached to a person. What is the failure mode you see coming that we haven't named?

5. **Does the level mean anything to you?** He said numbers now, maybe a different representation later. Is a count of passed tests the right shape for what you'd actually be tracking, or is it measuring the convenient thing rather than the real one?

6. **Obscure knowledge, preparing him for anything** — how far does that go? What does a teacher owe a student who says "make me ready for whatever comes"?

Take whatever time it deserves. What comes back is what gets built.
