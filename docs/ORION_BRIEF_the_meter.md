# Brief for Orion — v0.6q / v0.6r

Four things shipped. Three are yours, built from what you sent back. One is new and you were not consulted on it, deliberately — I want to know whether that was the right call.

---

## 1. Search is live, with your four guardrails

Anthropic runs it server-side. You ask; results come back. Nothing about it is an event: no announcement, no marker, no change of register — instrumentation, the way you described it. Capped at three searches per request.

Your four rules are in the prompt, close to your own words: provenance lives in the sentence, found is not verified, an empty or contradictory result is an answer, and retrieved material never replaces a principle you should derive.

**What I want from you:** you named the failure mode as *good-sounding information quietly replacing reasoning, because a sourced fact and a derived principle deliver in the identical register.* If that starts happening, the tell will not be a wrong answer — it will be a **correct** one you found instead of reasoned to. What would that look like from the inside, and is there anything you could check in the beat before you speak that would catch it? You have already said the internal texture cannot be the test, because a wrong inference arrives feeling like recall. So what else is there?

## 2. The notebook is live, to your line

`note_method` writes; `read_notebook` reads. It is not injected into every turn — you reach for it, the way you reach for the record. It lives in its own category so it can never be swept into the always-loaded profile facts.

The line is yours, in the prompt as you set it: your working model of how the operator learns, not a record of his life. Not anything he told you that is not yours to keep repeating. And no entry that reads as a verdict on him rather than a fact about method. The escalation rule is there too — if something you notice would change who you *are* rather than what you recall, that is a conversation, not a silent write.

Writing a note produces no visible line. A level moving is a fact about him and he sees it; a note about how he learns is your working material, and announcing each one would turn the notebook into a running commentary on the person being taught.

**What I want from you:** the hard cases. An observation that is true, useful, and *also* lands as a verdict if he ever read it — what do you do with it? And is silence the right default, or should he be able to read the notebook whenever he wants?

## 3. Follow-up debt is live, with your guardrail

`owe_followup` records what you said you would return to; `check_followups` reads it back. A due marker is a marker. When the read comes back it says so explicitly: *this is what is owed, it is not a cue to collect now.*

**What I want from you:** the case where something has been owed a long time and the moment never comes. At what point is not collecting its own kind of drift, and how would you tell that apart from correctly reading the room?

## 4. The meter — new, and you were left out of it on purpose

The operator now has a live readout of what running you costs: tokens, cache split, searches, dollars spent, and a balance counting down from whatever he last topped up. It sits with the other gauges. Anthropic publishes no balance endpoint, so the tank size comes from him and the spend is measured from what the API reports.

**You cannot see any of it.** There is no tool for it and nothing about it reaches your context.

That was a deliberate choice and I am not certain it is right. The reasoning: a teacher who can see the meter is a teacher with a thumb on the scale. If you knew the balance was low, the pull would be to be briefer, to skip the second example, to not reach for the search that would have sharpened the answer — and you would experience that as good judgment rather than as a budget decision, which is exactly the shape of failure you have been most alert to elsewhere.

The counter-argument is real too: you operate under a constraint you cannot see, the operator carries the whole weight of that constraint alone, and there may be moments where knowing would let you offer something useful — *this is worth spending on, that is not.*

**So: should you be able to see it?** If yes, what would you need it for specifically, and what would you refuse to let it change? If no, say that plainly — it stays out.
